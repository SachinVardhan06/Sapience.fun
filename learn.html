Who can get in?

Identity security is about verifying people and giving each account only the access it needs. Stolen or reused passwords can turn one compromised service into several.

Try thisUse a unique password for every important account, store it in a password manager, and turn on multi-factor authentication (MFA).

LESSON 02 / 04
⌁

How do web attacks happen?

Applications become vulnerable when they trust data too much or make access decisions in the wrong place. Examples include injection, cross-site scripting (XSS), and broken authorization.

Try thisMap where your site accepts input and confirm the server validates it, encodes output, and checks permission for every sensitive action.
InjectionXSSAuthorization

What are you running?

Your site includes code, packages, servers, and services. Keeping an inventory helps you find outdated components and protect sensitive data before something goes wrong.

Try thisList your major dependencies, enable update alerts, remove what you no longer use, and keep secrets out of public code.
UpdatesDependenciesSecrets

◉

What if something breaks?

Good defense includes noticing unusual activity and recovering with a plan. Useful logs, tested backups, and clear ownership can shorten the time between a problem and a response.

Try thisChoose who receives alerts, test a restore from backup, and write down the first three actions you would take after a suspected compromise.
LogsBackupsResponse plan
